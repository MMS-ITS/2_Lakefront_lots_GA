### 2.1 Is new private dock permitting OPEN or CLOSED?

**OPEN — but narrowly, and with a rule that may be decisive here.** [OFFICIAL]

There is **no general moratorium** on new private docks at Thurmond Lake in the 2018 SMP. The only use of the word "moratorium" in the plan is a *penalty* mechanism: non-compliance with permit terms "may also result in a moratorium, preventing the issuance of a new permit or license until compliance satisfactory to the Operations Project Manager has been achieved" (¶12.h). That is an enforcement tool against a specific permittee, not a lake-wide freeze.

What actually limits docks is **shoreline allocation plus a 2018 policy change**:

> **¶16.a — New Subdivisions.** "Only community docks will be authorized in all new county certified, platted subdivisions approved after implementation of this plan." Those developments must form an HOA or governing entity that deals with the Thurmond Lake Office directly; community dock placements are pre-determined; typically a **maximum of 2 slips per 82 feet** of Limited-Development-zoned shoreline. [OFFICIAL, SMP ¶16.a]

**Why this matters for Lots 130/131:** the Stillwater Coves Declaration was recorded **15 June 2006**, well before 1 May 2018. If the plat for the Watersedge Cove phase was county-certified pre-2018, individual private docks remain legally possible. If that phase (or a replat of it) was certified after 1 May 2018, **only a community dock is available and no individual dock will ever be permitted on these lots.** This is a **plat-date question that must be answered from the Lincoln County Clerk of Superior Court plat book before you buy.** [ACTION ITEM]

Your instinct about restricted permitting is also correct in a second sense: **~61–62% of the shoreline is closed to new private structures** by allocation (below).

### 2.4 Private dock permit requirements

**Eligibility (SMP ¶14):** [OFFICIAL]
- Applicant must own, **in fee simple**, private property **adjacent to public land that is zoned Limited Development**.
- **Minimum 20 feet of common frontage** with LD-zoned public land. Extremely shallow "finger" or "flag" lots **do not qualify even if they meet the 20-foot minimum**.
- **No easement access.** Applications are not accepted where access to public land is obtained through an easement granted by a private owner.
- Proof of access = recorded deed **and plat**.
- **A 90° projection survey is required.** A perpendicular projection from the shared private/public boundary determines the usable frontage area. **"If the 90° projection bisects or crosses other frontage areas, previously permitted areas, or private property before reaching the 330' msl, the property will not qualify for shoreline use permits."** The survey must be by a **state-licensed surveyor at 1 inch = 100 feet**, submitted with deeds and plats. *(Exhibit IV illustrates this.)*
- **¶14.d — two-lot trap:** if one structure (house, garage) or supporting feature (driveway, pool, porch) **occupies more than one lot, the lots are treated as ONE lot and qualify for ONE permit only.** If permits were already issued before such construction, the surplus permits are cancelled and those facilities must be removed. → **If you buy both lots and build a single house/driveway spanning them, you forfeit the second dock right.**
- No permits in wetlands, environmentally sensitive areas, or areas of cultural/historical/archaeological significance. No permits to minors.
- **¶12.e:** "Permits and/or licenses **will not be issued for commercial or speculative purposes in order to enhance the selling potential of adjacent private property.**" → A seller cannot obtain a dock permit to make a lot marketable.

**One dock per lot (¶15):** one private floating facility per **separate** piece of property, where "separate" means professionally surveyed, platted by a licensed surveyor, and **recorded as distinct in the county courthouse or tax office.** → Confirm from the plat/deed that 130 and 131 are two distinct recorded lots (the owner's ad and the realtor.com split records suggest they are).

**Dock size/type (¶15.a):**
- **Flat-T dock** (no slip): max **200 sq ft**, min **100 sq ft**, no side under 6 ft.
- **Dock with slip(s):** max **720 sq ft** total footprint including slip. Roof overhangs up to 24 in allowed. Sun decks, jet-ski lifts, staircases overhanging the bottom deck all count toward the footprint.
- Must be **certified by a licensed professional engineer**; **enclosed structures of any kind not authorized**; docks may not be used for human habitation; **houseboats and seaplanes may not be moored at private docks** (marina only).
- Flotation must be marine-grade, 100% warranted 8 years, fire resistant; drums/non-compartmentalized air containers prohibited.

**Spacing (¶15.c):**
- **Minimum 50-foot buffer** between the proposed facility and **any** existing facility or mooring buoy, measured at 330' msl between the two closest points.
- Dock **plus walkway must not extend more than one-third of the way across a cove** (measured 330'-msl shoreline to 330'-msl shoreline).
- **No "crossover"** placements beyond existing neighbouring facilities. A ranger decides placement, and may require a dock be put in **shallower** water rather than the most desirable spot.
- Where two or more docks could go in the same area but can't all meet spacing (**"small coves or narrow lots"**), it is **first-come, first-served**.

**Water depth minimum (¶15.d): THERE IS NO NUMERIC MINIMUM.** [OFFICIAL] The standard is qualitative: if the facility "would have sufficient water depth to be serviceable for **at least a part of the recreation season**," the application will be accepted for review. If approved in a shallow spot, a **Special Condition** is added recording that the applicant knew about shallow water. In low water, owners may "**chase water**" (relocate the dock temporarily) provided they don't block existing docks/coves or create hazards, and must return docks to permitted locations when levels recover. Temporary auger anchor pins below 330' are allowed in low water and must be removed as levels return.

**"Dockability" determination (FAQ):** [OFFICIAL]
> "Dockability" is outlined on page 7 of the SMP. It depends on shoreline zoning, location, and spacing from other structures. "**Each site is unique, and a field inspection is the only method to positively determine dock possibilities. The Thurmond Project Office encourages prospective buyers to request a field inspection prior to purchasing property on Thurmond Lake.**"

**Permit term and transfer:** [OFFICIAL]
- Term **up to 5 years**, non-transferable.
- **Automatically revoked on sale or transfer** of the adjacent private property, sale of the facility, or death of the permit holder and spouse.
- New owner must apply **within 14 days** of transfer, or the facility must be removed and the area restored within 14 days.
- Some facilities are "**grandfathered**" to the present owner only and **must be removed on sale** — the FAQ explicitly warns prospective buyers to check existing permits before buying.
- Appeal of a permit decision: in writing within 30 days through the Operations Project Manager to the District Commander.
- A **separate USACE Real Estate Licence** (Real Estate Division, ER 405-1-12) is required for land-based items that cross government land: improved walkways, electrical lines, potable and non-potable water lines, **dock gangwalks and dock anchors**. Revoking the licence forces revocation of the dock permit.

### 2.5 Is there a USACE fee-owned strip between private lots and the water, and how wide?

**Yes — and its width is variable, not fixed.** [OFFICIAL, FAQ + Boundary Line brochure]

- Land for the project was acquired under the **"Eisenhower Policy,"** which limited acquisition to what was needed for project purposes. The **prescribed purchase boundary was set at elevation 346 ft msl** — i.e. **16 feet of elevation above the 330-ft full pool.**
- The boundary **does not follow a true 346 contour.** Ground crews set iron pins and concrete monuments at elevations that captured the land needed, and **"with few exceptions, the government boundary follows straight lines between these pins/monuments."**
- Because the line runs straight pin-to-pin across varying topography, **"the distance between the shoreline and the boundary line varies more so than a true contour line."**
- **All land below the government property line is public land.** Orange/red tree markings are *witness* trees near — not on — the boundary. USACE urges a licensed survey to find the true line.
- **There is no Corps-required setback from the government boundary line**, but any unauthorized facility, activity or **encroachment over the boundary — including roof overhangs — will not be permitted and must be removed.**
- The MLS listing for a neighbouring Stillwater Coves lot states that from the back of the property the "**Corps Line to the lake is less than 200 feet**" [LISTING, 1230 Striper Cv], which is a plausible order of magnitude for this area.

**→ Practical answer:** expect a federal strip of roughly tens to a couple hundred feet of horizontal distance between the rear lot line and the 330-ft waterline, highly variable. **Get the surveyed distance for Lots 130/131 specifically.**

### 2.6 Are community/shared docks an option?

**Yes, and USACE actively prefers them.** [OFFICIAL, SMP ¶16]

- "In an effort to reduce impacts to public land, **community docks will be encouraged as the method of shoreline access and boat moorage on Thurmond Lake.**"
- A community dock = a structure with **two or more** mooring slips, optionally roofed, shared by more than one owner.
- **New (post-1 May 2018) county-certified subdivisions: community docks ONLY.** Requires an HOA/governing entity that works directly with the Thurmond Lake Office. Slip counts are set from the submitted plat — typically **max 2 slips per 82 ft** of LD-zoned shoreline. **The HOA decides which lots get moorage** and notifies USACE which lots it deems feasible. Community dock access corridors and parking must be on private property and shown on the final county-certified plat. **Slips cannot be made available to anyone outside the subdivision.**
- **Existing subdivisions:** community dock applications accepted only if slip count doesn't exceed the number of individual docks that would have been allowed; generally **one slip per 70 ft of dockable boundary line** (20-ft dock width + 50-ft spacing); one slip per dockable lot in the designated area.
- Sizing: first two slips combined ≤1,160 sq ft; each additional slip ≤454 sq ft; minimum two slips at initial installation; centre walkway 4–6 ft; slip fingers ≥4 ft; mooring buoys count as slips; uniform structure and materials.
- A sample **Community Dock Agreement** is Exhibit X of the SMP.
- **If a community corridor is the access point, ONLY a community dock may be authorized**, and it must sit within 200 ft of the community access point along the boundary line.

**How bedroom count is determined** [OFFICIAL, Section F ¶2.E]:

2. "**Soil absorption rates shall determine the absorption trench bottom area of the absorption field.**" The absorption rate comes from an on-site soil evaluation.
3. The system is sized to the **anticipated peak daily volume**, waste characteristics and soil characteristics at the field location.
4. Section B is blunt that desktop work isn't enough: "**Soil survey maps and reports prepared on the usual broad scales... are not sufficient to approve property for development with on-site sewage management systems. ### 9.1 Radon — **EPA Zone 3 (lowest), for both Lincoln and Wilkes Counties**

[OFFICIAL] *Georgia — EPA Map of Radon Zones*, https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2014-08/documents/georgia.pdf (index: https://www.epa.gov/radon/epa-maps-radon-zones-and-supporting-documents-state)

| Zone | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Zone 1 (red) | Predicted average indoor screening level **> 4 pCi/L** |
| Zone 2 (orange) | **≥ 2 and ≤ 4 pCi/L** |
| **Zone 3 (yellow)** | **< 2 pCi/L — lowest potential** |

EPA's own caveats, which you should respect: the map was built in 1993 from indoor measurements, geology, aerial radioactivity, soil parameters and foundation types; it is intended for targeting programs and radon-resistant building codes, **not** for deciding whether an individual home needs testing. **Relevance to a daylight-basement design:** the Design Guidelines encourage daylight basements, which are the highest-radon-risk part of a house. Zone 3 is reassuring but cheap passive radon-resistant construction (sub-slab membrane and a capped stack) is a sensible $1–2k at build.

### 9.2 Karst / sinkhole risk — **CONFIRMED LOW; this is crystalline Piedmont, not karst**

Three independent official lines of evidence:
1. **Soils/physiography:** "**Lincoln County is located in the Southern Piedmont soil region.** This is characterized by steep to gently rolling thin and well drained red soil with sandy loam surface layers over sandy clay to clay subsoils." [OFFICIAL] Lincoln County Joint Comprehensive Plan 2023–2028. The Piedmont province is crystalline/metamorphic (granite, gneiss, schist) — **no soluble carbonate bedrock, therefore no karst mechanism.** Georgia's karst and sinkhole problems are in the Coastal Plain and the Valley-and-Ridge, not the Piedmont.
2. **Hazard plan omission:** the *Lincoln County 2020 Multi-Hazard Pre-Disaster Mitigation Plan Update* — which systematically screens candidate hazards — **contains zero mentions of "sinkhole" or "karst."** The committee screened droughts, earthquakes, hail, lightning, hurricanes, extreme heat, severe winter storms, tropical storms, tornadoes, wildfire, dam failure and windstorms, and settled on **seven** hazards posing a direct, measurable threat: **flooding, dam failure, drought, wildfire, severe weather (tornadoes, tropical storms, thunderstorm winds, lightning, hail), winter storms, and earthquake.** Sinkholes never made the candidate list. [OFFICIAL] https://csrarc.ga.gov/sites/default/files/csrarc/planning/lincoln_county_mult-hazard_mitigation_plan.pdf
3. **Groundwater:** the Wilkes plan discusses **groundwater recharge areas** (fractured-rock recharge), which is the Piedmont pattern, not a carbonate-aquifer pattern. [OFFICIAL]

**Your premise is correct.** The practical geotechnical issue on these lots is **not** sinkholes; it is shallow rock, clay subsoils, slope, and the "**fair to poor suitability for septic tanks**" rating. Get a soils/geotech look at the building envelope, especially if a daylight basement means blasting or ripping rock.
